\subsection{Introduction}

The standard SO\cs2 product is taken from the 240-GHz retrieval (\texttt{CorePlusR3}). MLS can only measure significantly enhanced concentrations above nominal background such as that from volcanic injections. Validation of SO\cs2 is published in \citet{PumphreyEtal:2015}.

\subsection{Changes from v5}

Changes in the 240-GHz MLS products from v5 to v6 are minor. The standard SO\cs2 product is retrieved from the \texttt{CorePlusR3} phase that simultaneously retrieves tangent pressure, temperature, and its targeted species from 118- and 240-GHz signals. Changes made to improve the tangent pressure and temperature will have some impact on the SO\cs2 retrieval. Similarly, the new approach to cloud flagging discussed in section~\ref{sec:V6CloudWork} should be noted.

Figure~\ref{fig:avgmlsso2prflcomp} compares SO\cs2 in MLS v2.2, v3.3, v4.2, v5.0, and v6.0. The atmosphere typically has \mytexttilde0.1\,ppbv SO\cs2, which is far smaller than the MLS SO\cs2 accuracy estimate. All versions (erroneously) report typical abundance of order of a few ppbv, due to systematic errors in the MLS measurement system. The $-20\,\text{ppbv}$ reported at 215\,hPa is also a systematic artifact. Changes from v2 through to v6 involved handling of the background radiance signals which evolved from an absorption continuum (v2) to an RHi continnuum (v3) to a MLS limb-pointing-based absorption continuum with a frequency squared spectral dependence (v4--v6). Versions~4--6 also uses improved cloud detection and radiance screening.

\subsection{Resolution}
\onestandardavk{SO2}{SO\cs2}%

Based on Figure~\ref{fig:AvkSO2}, the vertical resolution for SO\cs2 is \mytexttilde3\,km and the horizontal resolution is 170\,km. The horizontal resolution perpendicular to the orbit track is 6\,km, the full width at half maximum of the MLS antenna, for all pressures.

\subsection{Precision}

The estimated precision for SO\cs2 is \mytexttilde4\,ppbv for all heights between 215--10\,hPa. The precisions are set to negative values (or zero in some cases) in situations when the retrieved precision is larger than 50\% of the a priori precision -- an indication that the data is biased toward the a~priori value. The changes between v5 and v6 are minor and result in only small differences in the estimated precision. An example of this is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:so2prec}. SO\cs2 precision from v5 and v6 show much less variability and less latitude dependence than was present in v4. Although only 100\,hPa is shown here, similar behavior is seen at other stratospheric levels and also in other species retrieved from the 240-GHz radiances, such as HNO\cs3. The change in behavior occurred as a result of reducing the forward model accuracy threshold from 0.02\,K to ``best possible'' in
v5 and v6.

\subsection{Accuracy}

In \prevv, SO\cs2 accuracy is estimated to be 10--20\,ppbv over the useful pressure range. This is not expected to change significantly for the v6 SO\cs2 product, for which accuracy estimates are yet to be updated.

\subsection{Data screening}
\label{sec:SO2Screening}
\begin{description}
  \item[Pressure range:] \screeningrule{215--10.0\,hPa} \standardrangestatement
  \item[Estimated Precision:] \screeningrule{Values with negative precision can be used, though with caution.}

    Although it is generally recommended to not use values where precision is flagged negative, SO\cs2 is an exception and it is appropriate to use values with negatively flagged precision (provided that the entire profile is not so flagged). High retrieved values of SO\cs2 at the larger pressures (e.g., 215 and 147\,hPa) will also have larger (i.e., poorer) precision values which are sometimes large enough to trigger the ``too much \emph{a priori} influence'' negative-precision flag. In these cases, there will be an \emph{a priori} influence, and the retrieved value will probably be smaller than reality because the retrieval is being pulled towards the \emph{a priori} value of zero ppbv. Nonetheless, this does not detract from the fact that greatly enhanced SO\cs2 is being observed, reflecting the detection of a plume. Profiles where the precision is set to zero, however, should be omitted from analyses.

    \evenstatusrule
  \item[Quality field:] \screeningrule{Only profiles having \texttt{Quality}\, \emph{greater} than 0.95 should be used.} As with water vapor, this value represents where the quality versus yield sharply drops indicating the transition from well fit profiles toward more poorly fit ones as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:so2datayields}
  \item[Convergence field:] \screeningrule{Only profiles having \texttt{Convergence}\, \emph{less} than 1.03 should be used.}
\end{description}

\subsection{Artifacts}

High values of SO\cs2 accompanied with negative errors are likely to be underestimated due to \emph{a priori} influence, which biases the value toward zero. The atmospheric background SO\cs2 (\mytexttilde0.1\,ppbv) cannot be measured by MLS, even with extensive data averaging, as there are systematic errors that induce biases of a few ppbv. The 215-hPa SO\cs2 has a background bias of approximately $-20\,$ppbv.

Figure~\ref{fig:so2overlay} shows an overlay comparison of column SO\cs2 measured by OMI and the same calculated by MLS for two days following the Sarychev eruption. It is clear that MLS detects the main plume dispersal features. It also appears that MLS columns are often smaller than those from OMI\@. Interpreting the significance of this is not straightforward given that OMI has to make assumptions regarding the profile shape and can observe SO\cs2 down to the boundary layer. The MLS column begins at 215\,hPa and integrates upward, thus neglecting the tropospheric contributions. Another limitation is that OMI can only make measurements during the day whereas MLS can make them day and night. Since the plume is moving relatively quickly over the 12-hour measurement separation time, MLS nighttime measurements often miss and/or detect plume features differently than OMI\@.

Figure~\ref{fig:so2timeseries} shows a comparison among v3, v4, v5, and v6 for a track showing the maximum SO\cs2 measured on 16~June 2009. As expected, v5 and v6 show similar amounts of SO\cs2 at levels 215--68\,hPa. Nominally the SO\cs2 is concentrated between 147 and 100\,hPa.
