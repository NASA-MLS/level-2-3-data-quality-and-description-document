\subsection{Introduction}

Geopotential height (GPH) is retrieved, along with temperature and the related assignment of tangent pressures to limb views, primarily from bands near \ch{O2} spectral lines at 118\,GHz and 234\,GHz. The \thisv GPH product is generally similar to the GPH products of earlier versions, including the \vii product that is described by \citet{SchwartzEtal:2008:Validation}, except that each profile is offset vertically in \thisv, as it was in \prevv, so that its value at 100\,hPa matches that of a global assimilation model. For \thisv, this reference level value is taken from \mbox{GEOS-5.29.4} ``Goddard Earth Observing System for Instrument Teams'' (\mbox{GEOS-IT}). In \viv and earlier versions, the absolute reference for GPH was inferred from the spacecraft orbit/attitude system and the instrument scan model. Throughout the rest of this section, ``\mbox{GEOS-5}'' without additional qualification refers to \mbox{GEOS-IT}.

The heights of surfaces of constant geopotential are a property of the Earth's gravitational field and do not depend upon atmospheric conditions. Geopotential differences between surfaces are equal to the integral with height of the gravitational acceleration, $g$. GPH is the geopotential difference from the Earth's surface geopotential to a given location, scaled by the mean-sea-level gravitational acceleration, $g_0$, to give units of height.

MLS products, including GPH and temperature, are reported on pressure surfaces, but pressure, temperature, and height depend upon one another through assumed hydrostatic balance and the gas law:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{g_o} \int g\,dh = \frac{R}{M g_o}\int T\,dP/P,
  \label{eq:GPHhydrostatic}
\end{equation}
where $M$ is the molar mass of air and $R$ is the gas constant.  If we assume that we know the molar-mass profile of the atmosphere (which begins to vary significantly from its dry-air surface value in the mesosphere due to height-dependent changes in the mixing ratios of \ch{O2} and \ch{N2}), then the retrieval of a temperature profile on fixed pressure surfaces also determines GPH differences between those surfaces, and so determines the complete GPH profile to within an additive constant. Absolute pointing cannot be inferred from radiometric information and must be obtained from a~priori information. In \viv and earlier versions, the spacecraft orbit/attitude system and instrument scan model provided this information, but there were systematic, annually repeating biases in the inferred \mbox{100-hPa} reference altitude that were large compared to expected error in GPH from assimilations such as \mbox{GEOS-5}.  In \thisv, \mbox{100-hPa} GPH from the \mbox{GEOS-IT} analysis is used as the reference height instead of values inferred from the instrument scan model.

\subsection{Differences between \thisv and previous versions}
Both \thisv and \prevv GPH products are taken from the \texttt{CorePlusR3} retrieval phase (along with a number of standard products that depend upon radiances from the 240\nobreakdash-GHz radiometer). The \mbox{GEOS-IT} \mbox{100-hPa} GPH, used by \thisv as its reference level, and \mbox{GEOS-FPIT} \mbox{100-hPa} GPH, used by \prevv, typically differ by less than 10\,m, so differences caused by the choice of reference level are negligible. 

The Earth Gravitational Model 1996 (EGM96) geoid defines zero height for \mbox{GEOS-5} GPH, \thisv GPH (which inherits this definition from \mbox{GEOS-5}), and \prevv GPH\@. Earlier versions of MLS GPH used the WGS84 ellipsoid that is used in the MLS forward model ray tracing. The impact of this difference is discussed in the \viv edition of this document \citep{EOSMLSV4Quality}.

In \prevv and \thisv, the fill value for missing GPH data (the geopotential heights themselves) has been changed to $-1\times10^{15}$\,m. The value of $-999.99$\,m used for missing GPH data in earlier versions of MLS GPH is within the range of valid GPH values that might be found at the bottom of a retrieval. The fill value for missing geolocation data (latitude, longitude, etc.) remains $-999.99$, consistent with that used for other products.

Figure~\ref{fig:GPHv5-GPHgeos_contourf}(a) shows biases in \thisv minus \prevv GPH that result from biases in \thisv minus \prevv temperature, shown in \ref{fig:v6v5_T_Res_Prec}(b). Relative to \prevv, \thisv has a low temperature bias of \mytexttilde$-2$\,K at 1\,hPa, a high bias of as much as $+3$\,K in the lower mesosphere, and a slightly low bias at higher levels. The \thisv minus \prevv GPH biases approach $-20$\,m at 1\,hPa, exceed $+100$\,m in the tropical middle mesosphere, and decline in magnitude at higher levels. 

\subsection{Comparison to GEOS-5 Analysis}
Figure~\ref{fig:GPHv5-GPHgeos_contourf}(b) shows that biases between \thisv and \mbox{GEOS-IT} GPH, which are zero by definition at 100\,hPa, grow through the stratosphere due to the persistent \mytexttilde1\,K low bias in \thisv temperature with respect to \mbox{GEOS-IT}.  In the lower and middle stratosphere, \mbox{GEOS-IT} assimilates numerous high-quality data sets, and it is likely that it is more accurate than MLS, so the differences shown in the lower parts of Figure~\ref{fig:GPHv5-GPHgeos_contourf}(b) are likely due to error in MLS GPH, not in \mbox{GEOS-IT}.  \mbox{GEOS-IT} is not well constrained by data in the mesosphere, so it is likely a less reliable correlative data set above 1\,hPa.

A latitudinally and seasonally varying bias between MLS \viv GPH and \mbox{GEOS-5} GPH was the subject of extensive discussion in the \viv edition of the MLS data quality document \citep{EOSMLSV4Quality}. Some of this annually repeating ``bias'' may result from annually repeating geophysical information captured by MLS that is not in the \mbox{GEOS-5} analysis. However, the large ascending/descending differences are almost certainly not primarily diurnal effects. The fidelity with which these patterns annually repeat suggests that they may result from thermal distortions of the spacecraft/instrument that vary annually due to the eccentricity of the Earth's orbit, or from orbit/attitude errors from the star trackers that provide the spacecraft-attitude information. The same star fields are viewed each year at the same date and orbit position.

Elimination of this bias was a primary motivation for the adoption of \mbox{GEOS-5} \mbox{100-hPa} GPH as an absolute reference height. The \mbox{GEOS-IT} operational products are available in time to be used in routine \thisv MLS processing and also provide the temperature a~priori for the retrieval. At 100\,hPa, the model of the near-real-time system is good enough that the differences that would accrue by waiting for a reanalysis with additional assimilated data (e.g., \mbox{MERRA-2}) are generally negligible compared to the GPH biases that were present in \viv.

In \thisv (as in \prevv), each profile is offset in height so that the \mbox{100-hPa} GPH reference level matches that of the \mbox{GEOS-5} GPH\@. Differences between \mbox{GEOS-5} GPH and \thisv GPH at other retrieval levels are the accumulated differences between \mbox{GEOS-5} and MLS temperatures as these differences are integrated (as in Equation~\ref{eq:GPHhydrostatic}) away from 100\,hPa. The \mbox{100-hPa} GPH produced by the MLS \thisv retrieval algorithm is stored as a diagnostic swath in the ``DGG'' file before is is overwritten with \mbox{GEOS-5} values.

\subsection{Vertical resolution}

The GPH profile is vertically integrated temperature, so its vertical resolution is not well defined. The vertical resolution of the underlying temperature given in Section~\ref{sec:StandardTemperature} is repeated in Table~\ref{tab:GPHSummary}.

\subsection{Precision}

MLS \thisv GPH precision is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:GPHSummary}. Precision is the random component of measurements that will average down if a measurement is repeated. The retrieval algorithms produce an estimate of GPH precision only for the \mbox{100-hPa} reference level, as this is the only element included in the MLS ``state vector.'' In \thisv, this level is replaced by the \mbox{GEOS-5} \mbox{100-hPa} GPH, and so has no random error from MLS radiance noise, leading to the 0\,m precision reported for 100\,hPa in Table~\ref{tab:GPHSummary}. Precisions at other standard-product profile levels (summarized in the second column of Table~\ref{tab:GPHSummary}) are calculated from the profiles of temperature precisions.  \thisv GPH precision is improved (smaller) in the upper stratosphere and mesosphere compared to that of \prevv due to improvements in the temperature retrieval precision, with typical reductions exceeding 15\% (e.g.\ 38\,m vs.\ 46\,m at 0.1~hPa) in the lower mesosphere. Typical estimated precisions remain smaller than 20\,m up to 1\,hPa and degrade to \mytexttilde100\,m at 0.01\,hPa. Off-diagonal elements of the temperature/GPH error covariance matrix are neglected in this GPH-precision-profile calculation, but resulting errors are believed to be small.

\subsection{Accuracy}

The \thisv GPH accuracy has been modeled through consideration of a variety of sources of systematic error, much as \vii GPH accuracy was modeled, as discussed by \citet{SchwartzEtal:2008:Validation}. Results of these calculations are given in the fourth column of Table~\ref{tab:GPHSummary}. Substantial biases believed to originate in satellite pointing data and/or time-varying biases in the instrument pointing system have been removed through the use of \mbox{GEOS-5} \mbox{100-hPa} GPH as an absolute GPH reference rather than information from the satellite attitude and instrument scan model. Of the remaining error sources considered, modeled amplifier non-linearity has the largest impact, just as is the case with the calculation for temperature.

``Observed accuracy'' in Table~\ref{tab:GPHSummary} is an estimate of the bias based upon comparisons with analyses and with other previously validated satellite-based measurements.

\subsection{Known Artifacts}
GPH in \prevv and earlier versions had large artifacts, reaching \mytexttilde3500\,m in amplitude, in the orbit following ``inclination adjustment maneuvers'' (IAMs), which are done to keep Aura in the ``A-Train'' constellation of satellites. Starting in \prevv, IAM-related biases were eliminated by the use of \mbox{GEOS-5} \mbox{100-hPa} GPH as an absolute reference.

\subsection{Data screening}
\label{sec:GPHScreening}
GPH should be screened in much the same way as is temperature:
\begin{description}
  \item[Pressure range:] \screeningrule{261--0.00046\,hPa.} \standardrangestatement

    \standardprecisionrule{} For the hydrostatic integration calculations that compute GPH above and below the \mbox{100-hPa} reference level, GPH precision is set negative at any level where the temperature precision is negative.  Furthermore, the output GPH precision remains negative for all levels beyond (i.e., further above or further below), even if the temperature precision becomes positive again.

    \evenstatusrule

  \item[Clouds:] Thick clouds are believed have some impact on \thisv GPH measurements in the upper troposphere (261--100\,hPa). If a user wants to prioritize minimization of the impact of clouds on GPH in the troposphere, GPH may be screened using the ice water content (IWC) product, rejecting profiles between 261 and 100\,hPa for which the 215\,hPa value of IWC is greater than 0.005\,g/m$^3$.

  \item[Quality:] Only use profiles with \texttt{Quality} greater than 0.2 for the 83\,hPa level and smaller pressures, and profiles with \texttt{Quality} greater than 0.9 at pressures of 100\,hPa and larger.

  \item[Convergence:] \convergencerule{1.03} Use of this threshold typically discards less than 0.1\% of profiles and is primarily a safeguard against profiles with extremely poor convergence.

\end{description}
\end{nofloatpages}
