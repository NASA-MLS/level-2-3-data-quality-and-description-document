\subsection{Introduction}

The standard water vapor product is taken from the 190\nobreakdash-GHz ``\texttt{CorePlusR2}'' retrieval. The vertical grid for \ch{H2O} is 12 levels per decade change in pressure (LPD) for 1000--1\,hPa, 6\,LPD for 1.0--0.1\,hPa, and 3\,LPD for 0.1--10\cp{$-5$}\,hPa. The horizontal grid is every 1.5\degsym\ along the orbit track. \ch{H2O} is unusual among MLS products in that it is assumed that the logarithm of the volume mixing ratio (VMR), and not mixing ratio itself, varies linearly with log-pressure; however, \ch{H2O} is still retrieved in VMR (not $\log$ VMR) just as is the case for the other MLS constituents. This means that, when interpolating MLS measurements to other locations (along track and/or vertically), such interpolation should be performed linearly on the logarithm of the \ch{H2O} concentration (VMR).

The recommended vertical range for MLS \thisv \ch{H2O} is 316--0.001\,hPa.  The uppermost retrieved level, 0.00046\,hPa, is not vertically resolved from the levels above it and should be regarded as a measure of the total column water vapor at and above this level. The 0.00046\nobreakdash-hPa data may be usable for some studies when interpreted in this manner, but only in consultation with the MLS science team.  The MLS \ch{H2O} data between 1000 and 383\,hPa are taken from a retrieval of relative humidity with respect to ice (RHi), converted to specific humidity using the Goff-Gratch vapor pressure over ice equation. This RHi retrieval is not vertically resolved, and all levels between 1000 and 383\,hPa are assumed to have the same RHi. See Section~\ref{sec:StandardRHI} for more information. \ch{H2O} for pressures less than or equal to 0.000215\,hPa are filled with \emph{a priori} values.

Validation of MLS \vii water vapor is presented in \citet{ReadEtal:2007} and \citet{LambertEtal:2007}. This section reiterates the key information from those studies, and updates them for \thisv. Table~\ref{tab:H2OSummary} gives a summary of MLS \thisv \ch{H2O} precision, resolution, and accuracy.

\subsection{Changes from \viv to \prevv and \thisv}
\label{sec:H2OChanges}

\subsubsection{Modifications to the \ch{H2O} retrievals in \thisv}

A defect in MLS \viv and prior versions was that \ch{H2O} exhibited a rather low bias (20--50\%) around  2--3\,km below the tropopause \citet{ReadEtal:2022}. Another deficiency was that that version likely underestimated the \ch{H2O} seasonal cycle at 147\,hPa in the tropics. These two issues are related. The tropopause height has a strong seasonal cycle in the tropics, and the dry bias at a given height will be modulated accordingly. This acted to flatten the seasonal cycle in retrieved 147\,hPa \ch{H2O} in earlier MLS versions. Additionally, it is clear that the MLS 190\nobreakdash-GHz radiometer that measures \ch{H2O} (and other species, notably \ch{N2O} and upper stratospheric \ch{HNO3})  is slowly drifting, due to aging of this instrument subsystem. A possible drift in the MLS \ch{H2O} was first noted by \citet{HurstEtal:2016}. An in depth investigation into potential causes for all these issues \citep{LiveseyEtal:2021:Drifts} has shown the most likely contributors to be:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A slow (\mytexttilde2\%-per-decade) drift in the 190\nobreakdash-GHz ``sideband fraction'' (the relative sensitivity of the radiometer to signals either side of the 190\nobreakdash-GHz ``local oscillator'' tone),
\item An offset between the pre-launch-measured value of that sideband fraction and the post-launch value (likely due to a difference in instrument operating temperature between the two environments and the radiometer being more temperature sensitive than designed), and
\item A bias (within the uncertainty estimated for pre-launch instrument alignment measurements) in the assumed pointing offset between the 190\nobreakdash-GHz radiometer and other MLS radiometers.
\end{enumerate}

The \prevv algorithms included a correction for these issues, including a time-dependent sideband fraction estimate derived internally from the MLS observations (with atmospheric reanalysis temperatures being the only external input).  Importantly, as described in more detail in \citet{LiveseyEtal:2021}, this correction makes no reference to correlative measurements of stratospheric water vapor from satellite or ballon observations, although improved agreement with such correlative measurements builds confidence in the validity of the correction.

The same correction is applied in a more-or-less identical manner in \thisv. In version 5, the correction was based on a version 4 band 2 opacity calculation that used the pre-launch sideband fraction for computing the correction. This requires that version 4 also be available alongside version 5 processing. In version 6 The correction is based on the band 2 opacity calculation using the sideband fraction that was used for the calculation, typically the sideband fraction in effect for the prior month, as this quantity is updated monthly. This change was implemented such that updating the sideband fraction can be done within the version being processed. In other words the version 6 processing does not require version 5 or 4 processing to be going on concurrently. The difference in the two methods are negligibly small on the order of less than 0.01\% in the correction. Of note is that version 4 processing was halted on May 31, 2024 but version 5 processing continues to the present day. Beginning on 1 June 2024 version 5 processing used sideband fractions based on the v6 sideband calculation. Another difference between \prevv and \thisv is that limb tangent pressure is no longer retrieved from the \ch{H2O} linewidth in \thisv. Instead, the tangent pressure estimated from the \ch{O2} measurements in an earlier phase of processing is used.  This change was enacted because:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item there is a small drift (equivalent to a \mytexttilde20\,m change in altitude over the mission lifetime) between the tangent pressures retrieved from the \texttt{CorePlusR3} and \texttt{CorePlusR2} phases, and
  \item in the immediate aftermath of the January 2022 Hunga eruption, the tangent pressure retrieved from the \texttt{CorePlusR2} (\ch{H2O}) phase failed, given the unprecedented levels of \ch{H2O} and SO\cs2 in the atmosphere at that time.  This forced users to revert to \viv retrievals of \ch{H2O} in the vicinity of the Hunga plume for a month after that event.
\end{enumerate}
The cause for the 20\,m drift between the two tangent pressure estimates is not fully understood, but may originate from subtle aspects of the method used to estimate the 190\nobreakdash-GHz sideband fraction drift in \prevv (which derives the correction using \viv data).  A new approach for estimating the sideband fraction drift was developed for \thisv (and for v5.04, which can no longer use \viv input data now that MLS \viv processing is discontinued).  The differences in these two sideband fraction drift estimates are at most 0.01\%, and impacts of this difference on the \ch{H2O} product can be considered negligible.  One final change between \prevv and \thisv that impacts the \ch{H2O} product results from efforts to place the retrievals of \ch{N2O} on a sounder footing.  These are described in more detail in Sections~\ref{sec:V6N2OCorrectionBriefSummary} and~\ref{sec:V6N2OCorrectionDetails}.

As for \prevv, humidity data at pressures greater than 316\,hPa are derived from a broad layer relative humidity retrieval (using low limb viewing MLS wing channel radiances), this is similar means that used for NOAA operational humidity sounders such as TOVS\@. As noted in \citet{ReadEtal:2007}, the \vii \ch{H2O} retrievals at pressures larger than 316\,hPa were likely to be \mytexttilde30\% too high, based on comparisons with AIRS\@. The accuracy of this retrieval is highly sensitive to the transmission efficiency of the MLS optics system. In \viv and thereafter, the assumed value of the MLS antenna transmission efficiency was adjusted empirically (within the uncertainty range established from MLS pre-launch calibration) to give better agreement with AIRS in the tropics. Also in \viv and later versions, the assumed \ch{N2} continuum emission/absorption coefficient was adjusted (only for this preliminary relative humidity retrieval phase), to minimize the clear sky cloud induced radiance bias. This retrieval is used as an \emph{a priori} and profile constraint for the humidity profile at pressures greater than 316\,hPa, which are not retrieved in the standard \ch{H2O} product retrieval.

The number of ``good'' \ch{H2O} profiles has declined slightly over the mission life. A typical day has 3495 available profiles and, depending on the season, the number passing the quality screens runs from 95--99\%, corresponding to 35--175 of profiles being rejected per day. Most of these rejections are caused by poor radiance fits (quality $<0.7$). Over the course of the mission, for \viv this has increased by \mytexttilde25 profiles per day. The changes in \prevv reduced this trend to only 8 profiles per day. For \thisv, this difference between the start of the mission and the most recent data has increased slightly to 10 profiles per day.

\subsubsection{Impacts of these changes on the MLS \ch{H2O} dataset}

The \thisv \ch{H2O} product agrees with \prevv to within 2\% in the bulk of the stratosphere and in the mesosphere.  Lower down, \thisv is 2--3\% wetter than \prevv at 68\,hPa, 5--10\% wetter at 83 and 100\,hPa, 5--10\% drier at 121 and 178\,hPa, and 5--10\% wetter between 215--316\,hPa. Also, at 10--8.3\,hPa, \thisv is smoother than \prevv as a result of changes to the manner in which the \emph{a priori} profile is constructed.

Notable changes are also seen in the upper troposphere, where the estimated precision roughly scales by the amount of \ch{H2O}\@. Higher concentrations of \ch{H2O} are typically accompanied by larger (i.e., worse) estimated precision (in VMR); however, in terms of percent of concentration the precision value declines somewhat. Taken together, the large dynamic range of possible humidity in the upper troposphere, combined with the non-linear behavior of the instrument response to these concentrations, are the main motivation for choosing a logarithmic representation for \ch{H2O} in the retrievals. Differences in precision between \vii and later versions result from the use of a coarser retrieval grid above 22\,hPa and a different value of the \ch{H2O} linewidth.

\subsection{Resolution}
\onestandardavk{H2O_HR}{\ch{H2O}}%

The spatial resolution is obtained from examination of the averaging kernel matrices shown in Figure~\ref{fig:AvkH2O_HR}. The vertical resolution for \ch{H2O} is in the range 1.3--3.6\,km from 316--0.22\,hPa and degrades to 6--11\,km for pressures lower than 0.22\,hPa. The along track horizontal resolution is \mytexttilde170--350\,km for pressures greater than 4.6\,hPa, and degrades to 400--740\,km at smaller pressures. The resolutions shown in Table~\ref{tab:H2OSummary} are averages of the equatorial and 70\degsym N values given in Figure~\ref{fig:AvkH2O_HR}. The horizontal cross-track resolution is 9\,km, the width of the MLS 190\nobreakdash-GHz field-of-view for all pressures. The longitudinal separation of the MLS measurements is 10\degsym--20\degsym\ over middle and lower latitudes, with much finer sampling in polar regions. The instrument nominally scans to 0.001\,hPa, therefore the retrieved value at 0.00046\,hPa is not vertically resolved and is effectively a column measurement from 0.001\,hPa to the spacecraft, with the \ch{H2O} profile values for $p \le 0.000215\,$hPa fixed at \emph{a priori} values.

\subsection{Precision}

Table~\ref{tab:H2OSummary} summarizes the estimated precision of the MLS \thisv \ch{H2O} data. For pressures $\geq100\,$hPa, the precisions quoted are the 1$\sigma$ scatter about the mean of coincident comparison differences, which are larger than the formal retrieval precisions estimated by the Level~2 software \citep{ReadEtal:2007}. For $p\leq83\,$hPa the precisions are the 1$\sigma$ scatter of coincident ascending/descending MLS profile differences \citep{LambertEtal:2007}. The individual Level~2 precisions are set to negative values or zero in situations when the retrieved precision is larger than 50\% of the a priori precision -- an indication that the data are biased toward the a priori value.

\subsection{Accuracy}

The values for accuracy are based on a full systematic error analysis performed on the MLS measurement system as described (for v2) in \citet{ReadEtal:2007} and \citet{LambertEtal:2007}. The results are shown in Table~\ref{tab:H2OSummary}. For pressures between 316 and 178\,hPa, comparisons between AIRS v7 and MLS \thisv show agreement within 20\% in the zonal mean (with MLS usually drier for low concentrations and wetter for high concentrations) equatorward of 40\degsym, with MLS having a dry biases at higher latitudes.  The values in Table~\ref{tab:H2OSummary} for pressures between 316 and 178\,hPa from the systematic errors analysis suggest that those values are pessimistic relative to that seen with comparisons with AIRS validated accuracies. For the pressure range 178--83\,hPa, the quoted values come directly from the systematic error analysis performed on the MLS measurement system.

\subsection{A note about the water vapor averaging kernels}

In general, averaging kernels are most applicable to linear and moderately non-linear retrieval problems. The MLS \ch{H2O} retrievals are mostly linear, except when the atmosphere approaches opaqueness at the frequencies observed by MLS\@. This occurs when the limb tangent of the instrument field of view is less than 10\,km above the Earth's surface and the atmosphere is moist. In such cases the measurements are very nonlinear and often times the averaging kernel calculations for the lowest retrieved levels are numerically unstable and unrepresentative of the actual MLS measurement system. This is the case, for example, for the 316\,hPa and 262\,hPa equatorial kernels shown in Figure~\ref{fig:AvkH2O_HR}. Analysis of simulated MLS observations indicates that application of the averaging kernel provides little benefit to analyses involving the 316\,hPa and 262\,hPa levels, and accordingly we recommend not applying the (unrepresentative) kernels at those levels.

\subsection{Data screening}
\label{sec:H2OScreening}
\begin{description}
  \item[Pressure range:] \screeningrule{316--0.001\,hPa.} \standardrangestatement\ Data at 0.00046\,hPa represents a total column at and above that pressure level. Scientific use of these 0.00046\,hPa data may be possible, but requires consultation with the MLS team.
  \standardprecisionrule
  \evenstatusrule
  \item[Clouds:] Profiles flagged as being affected by high or low clouds (i.e., with \texttt{Status} bits corresponding to values of \texttt{16} and \texttt{32} set) can continue to be used. See artifacts for more details.
  \item[Quality field:] \screeningrule{Only profiles with a value of the \texttt{Quality} \emph{greater} than 0.7 should be used in scientific studies.} The quality threshold is deliberately set low because it is not a very good discriminator of poor profiles except when quality is well below the mean value; therefore, we recommend the ``additional screening to avoid outliers,'' as described below.
  \item[Convergence field:] \screeningrule{Only profiles with a value of the \texttt{Convergence} \emph{less} than 2.0 should be used in scientific studies.}
  \item[Additional screening to avoid outliers:] After applying the \texttt{Status}, \texttt{Quality}, and \texttt{Convergence} screening described above, some unphysical profiles remain in the MLS \thisv\ \ch{H2O} dataset. These are characterized by unrealistically low water vapor mixing ratios in the upper troposphere and stratosphere. The cause behind this behavior is from using the logarithmic basis which excludes negative values. Sometimes the retrieval minimizer seeks a negative value at a given level (which is almost always accompanied by severe vertical profile oscillations). Such values are not allowed and therefore an optimal fit to the measurement signal is not achieved. \ch{H2O} profiles having values less than 0.101\,ppmv (0.101$\times 10^{-6}$\,VMR) at any pressure at or larger than 1\,hPa should not be used. Profiles with acceptable \texttt{Status}, \texttt{Quality}, and \texttt{Convergence} having values less than 0.101\,ppmv at pressures $< 1$\,hPa (vertical levels 38--55) are acceptable and occur frequently, because the water vapor concentration in the mesosphere is often less than the measurement precision and negative values are preferable but not realizable due to the logarithmic basis definition being used.

\end{description}

\subsection{Artifacts}

There is a minimum concentration where MLS \ch{H2O} measurements at a given level become unreliable. This is given in Table~\ref{tab:H2OSummary} under the ``Min.\ \ch{H2O}'' column. The lowest allowable \ch{H2O} value is 0.1\,ppmv. Differences between the retrieved middle tropospheric \ch{H2O} constraint and the true atmospheric state can cause errors at 316 and 261\,hPa. The error manifests either as dry ($<1$\,ppmv) or moist spikes in an orbital time series. Such data are often accompanied with good \texttt{Quality} and \texttt{Status} but should be rejected by checking the profile for such events on a level-by-level basis; there is no need to reject other levels in a given profile if one level has values smaller than this threshold.

Clouds in the field of view degrade the data in unpredictable ways. Many instances of \texttt{Quality} $<0.7$ occur in the presence of clouds where the cloud screening of the radiances failed to reject them. Cloud radiative scattering distorts the spectral lineshape, causing poor fits and low quality values. However, not all MLS signals are obviously affected. Coincident comparisons of MLS cloud-flagged \ch{H2O} (\texttt{Status} bits corresponding to values of \texttt{16} or \texttt{32} set between 316--215\,hPa, considering only profiles with acceptable values of \texttt{Quality}) with AIRS show a small mean bias of 10\% but exhibit a 50\% increase in variability for the individual differences. Users should therefore be aware that, although the overall biases for measurements inside clouds are similar to that for clear sky, individual profiles affected by cloud will exhibit greater variability about the true atmospheric humidity than those not marked as being cloud-influenced.

As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:H2OChanges} (and~\ref{sec:R2DutyCycling}), the behavior of the MLS 190\nobreakdash-GHz radiometer is slowly drifting.  While \thisv (as with \prevv) applies a correction for this drift, some signature of such a drift remains, particularly in comparison to frostpoint hygrometer observations.

In the mesosphere, the random error (precision) is often larger than the retrieved value and therefore negative concentrations are possible and proper. However, the logarithmic definition of the \ch{H2O} basis doesn't allow retrieval of negative values, with the minimum lowest allowed concentration being 0.1\,ppmv. Therefore an average of many profiles will produce a positive bias. It is recommended that a better representation of the average of many profiles is achieved by calculating the associated median.

The MLS limb scan nominally tops out around 0.001\,hPa. Therefore, the 0.00046\,hPa, which is now retrieved in \thisv, is not a vertically resolved measurement but rather a column amount from 0.001\,hPa to the spacecraft altitude, under the constraint that all levels above 0.00046\,hPa are fixed at \emph{a priori} values. Accordingly, should there be a large \ch{H2O} event in any level above 0.00046\,hPa, it would be retrieved as an enhancement at 0.00046\,hPa, but its true vertical location above 0.00046\,hPa would be unknown.

\subsection{Desired improvements should another data version be produced}

While many defects in \viv and earlier versions have been addressed, further investigation of their effectiveness, especially the drift reduction and high latitude low value retrievals in the upper troposphere is possible, and improvements may be theoretically feasible. Also, it may be possible to obtain a vertically resolved \ch{H2O} product for pressures greater than 316\,hPa at high latitudes during dry conditions.
