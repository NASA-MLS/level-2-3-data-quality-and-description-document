\subsection*{Introduction}

\subsubsection*{Aging of the MLS 640\,GHz N\cs2O signals}
The standard product for \thisv N\cs2O for the period from launch until
June 26, 2013, is taken from the 640 GHz
(`Core+R4B') retrieval (N2O-640) and details of the retrieval method and
validation results are presented in \citep{LambertEtal07}.

Analysis of band 12 (640-GHz) housekeeping data during 2013 showed
declining output in counts, increasing radiance noise (variance) and
also increasing correlation between the band 12 radiance channels. As
the \thisv N\cs2O product began to show 
further deterioration in quality a decision was made to turn off band 12
on August 6, 2013.

The standard product for \thisv N\cs2O for the period following June 26, 2013,
is taken from the 190 GHz (`CorePlusR2') retrieval (N2O-190). The 190 GHz N\cs2O 
data show slightly worse precision and resolution compared to the 640 GHz
retrievals. Data from N2O-640 and N2O-190 have been compared from launch until the 
end of band 12 operations. There is a persistent high bias in N\cs2O-190 of 30\% at
100 hPa and a smaller bias of 5--10\% at 68 hPa 
compared to N\cs2O--640. From 46 to 3.2 hPa the biases are less than 5\%.

Other significant differences between the \thisv N2O-640 and N2O-19O
data such as the resolution, precision, 
quality and convergence criteria are noted below.

\subsubsection*{Changes from v2.2 for the pre-June 2013 N20-640 data}
All of the \thisv 640-GHz retrieval phases use the temperature and tangent pressure
information from and earlier retrieval phase, instead of including a
joint temperature/pTan retrieval as was done for v2.2.  This change has
significantly reduced the number of `non convergent' 640-GHz retrievals
in \thisv compared to v2.2.

\subsection*{Resolution}
\onestandardavk{N2O-640}{640\,GHz N\cs2O}%

\onestandardavk{N2O}{190\,GHz N\cs2O}

The spatial resolution reported by the averaging kernel matrices shown
in Figures~\ref{fig:AvkN2O} for the 640\,GHz measurements (pre
June~26, 2013) and~\ref{fig:AvkN2O-190} for the 190\,GHz measurements
(post June~26, 2013).  For N2O-640 , the vertical resolution is
4\,--\,6\,km and the horizontal along-track resolution is
300\,--\,600\,km over most of the useful range of the retrievals. For
N2O-190, the vertical resolution is 4\,--\,8\,km and the horizontal
along-track resolution is 300\,--\,600\,km.

The horizontal cross-track
resolution is set by the 3\,km width of the MLS 640-GHz field-of-view for
all pressures (c.f. 7\,km width for the MLS 190-GHz field-of-view).  
The longitudinal separation of the MLS measurements is
10\degsym--\,20\degsym\ over middle and lower latitudes, with much
finer sampling in polar regions.

\subsection*{Precision}
Precision as defined here is the 1-$\sigma$ uncertainty in the retrieved
value calculated by the Level~2 algorithms and has been validated
against the scatter about the mean of coincident ascending/descending
MLS profile differences.  The estimated precision on a single retrieved
profile given in Table~\ref{tab:N2OSummary} varies with height from
\texttildelow12\,--\,24\,ppbv for N2O-640 and \texttildelow15\,--\,21\,ppbv
for N2O-190.

The N\cs2O values at the 147 hPa pressure level have
a large a priori influence and practically all precisions are flagged
negative at this level.

\subsection*{Accuracy}

The `accuracy' values given in Table~\ref{tab:N2OSummary} are taken from
the detailed analysis presented of MLS 640-GHz v2.2 data in
\citet{LambertEtal07} to quantify the systematic uncertainties
associated with the MLS instrument calibration, spectroscopic
uncertainty and approximations in the retrieval formulation and
implementation.  Accuracy of \thisv N\cs2O data from the 640\,GHz
signals (pre June 26, 2013 observations) are expected to be little
different from that established for v2.2.  Accuracy for the post June

\item[Convergence (N\cs2O-190, pre June 26, 2013):] \convergencerule{5.0}

  A fraction of the N\cs2O-190 data (typically less than 2\%) at this level
  will be discarded via this screening.

\item[Quality (N\cs2O-190, post June 26, 2013):] \qualityrule{1.0}
  
  A small fraction of N\cs2O-640 profiles (typically less than 0.5\%) will
  be discarded via this screening.

\item[Clouds:] \screeningrule{Clouds can impact the N\cs2O product at
    the lowest altitudes.  See below for details.}

  Very thick clouds in the tropics produce a low rate of artifacts in
  the N\cs2O product, consisting of abnormally high (and, more rarely,
  low) values at 100\,hPa (and 147\,hPa, not recommended for scientific
  use).  Such cases are not always detected by the \texttt{Quality} and
  \texttt{Convergence} flags, and the cloud bits of the \texttt{Status}
  field are too blunt a tool to identify these cases, needlessly
  discarding reasonable data.  We recommend checking for the occurrence
  of N\cs2O values greater than 350\,ppbv on the 68\,hPa MLS retrieval
  level in order to remove significant outliers from the
  100\,--\,46\,hPa data.

\end{description}

\subsection*{Artifacts}

The \thisv N\cs2O retrievals are improved at 100\,--\,68\,hPa, where the
v2.2 retrievals often showed signs of poor convergence resulting in sets
of consecutive `smooth' profiles. There are occasional nonphysical
values of N\cs2O in the \thisv data, and screening using the convergence
and quality fields (see above) is recommended to remove the majority of
these data points.

The retrieval restricts N\cs2O values to be greater than $-$40 ppbv
(approximately three times the retrieval noise level in the recommended
pressure range) in order to prevent problems in the minimization search
process.  The low bound is applied at all levels, but it is only evident
in the data for pressures less than 0.1\,hPa, where the vertical
smoothing is relaxed and the retrieval noise becomes comparable to the
magnitude of the low bound value.  Accordingly, statistical averaging of
the data (zonal means or longer time periods) cannot be applied
successfully for pressures at and less than 0.1\,hPa as the $-$40\,ppbv
hard limit introduces a positive bias in any average.

\subsection*{Review of comparisons with other datasets}

Average values for \thisv N\cs2O are 20\% larger than in v2.2 for the
100\,hPa pressure level, up to 10\% smaller at the 46\,--\,32\,hPa
levels, and within 5\% for pressures greater than 22\,hPa (see
Figure~\ref{fig:N2OComparison}).

Apart from the differences noted above, the MLS \thisv N\cs2O is similar
to the MLS v2.2 product described and validated in
\citet{LambertEtal07}.  Comparisons of v2.2 N\cs2O with coincident
measuremements by ACE-FTS, Odin/SMR, and Envisat/MIPAS and balloon borne
observations are shown in \citet{LambertEtal07}.  A revised validation
paper for N\cs2O is not planned in the near future and users are
encouraged to read \citet{LambertEtal07} for more information.
